      New to company accounts? Start here

      Every UK limited company publishes its accounts for free on Companies House. They're full of useful facts, like how much the company sells, whether it makes a profit and how much cash it holds, but they're hard to read if you're not an accountant. TagTrace lets you ask for those facts in everyday words.

      What is an iXBRL filing?

      It's the annual report saved as a web page, with a hidden tag on every number saying exactly what it is (for example, profit before tax for the year ended 31 December 2025). Most UK companies now file their accounts this way.

      Because every figure is labelled, TagTrace can look up the exact number. It doesn't have to hope an AI read the right line of a 200-page document.

      How TagTrace answers a question

      1. Read the tags

        The filing is read directly and every tagged figure is extracted with its units, sign and scale (so a figure shown in £m becomes the real number). Nothing is downloaded from anywhere else.

      2. Store every version

        Each figure is kept with its concept, period and any breakdown. When a filing tags the same concept several times, for example this year, last year and by segment, all of them are kept.

      3. Find the right figures

        Your question is matched to the concepts it's about, and only those figures go to the AI. That's a few dozen facts rather than a slice of a report that can run to hundreds of pages.

      4. Check before showing

        The AI has to name the figures it used. The server looks the value up itself, does any arithmetic itself, and checks that any quote really appears in the filing.

      What the badges mean

      • Matched to tagged fact read straight from one tagged figure
      • Computed from tagged facts the server did the maths, not the AI
      • Quote found in filing the quoted sentence appears word for word
      • Not verified the AI's answer couldn't be confirmed, so treat it with care
      • Not in tagged data nothing in the filing answers it, so no number is made up

      What it can't do

      • It can confirm a number is in the filing, but not that it's the right one. Check the period and breakdown on each card.
      • It only knows what's in the file you upload. It doesn't know about news, share prices or other years' accounts.
      • It can't read scanned or PDF-only accounts.
      • It isn't financial advice.

      Where your data goes

      • Your file is deleted as soon as it has been read.
      • The extracted figures are deleted when you clear the filing, or after 30 minutes of inactivity.
      • Your question and the matching figures are sent to OpenAI or Anthropic to write the answer.
      • If you rate an answer, the question, the answer and your vote are logged to measure accuracy. Nothing identifies you.
      • No sign-up, no analytics and no adverts.

      Does it work? The research behind it

      TagTrace comes from my MSc Artificial Intelligence dissertation on detecting and reducing AI hallucinations in UK iXBRL financial reports. I tested two approaches on 102 questions about 12 real UK filings: letting the AI read the report text, and giving it the tagged figures instead.

      102-question benchmark · 12 UK filings (8 FTSE 350 under IFRS, 4 private companies under FRS 102)
      ApproachCorrectWrongNo answer
      Claude · reads report text44.1%453
      Claude · tagged figures92.2%53
      GPT-5.6 Terra · reads report text41.2%555
      GPT-5.6 Terra · tagged figures79.4%183

      Giving the AI the right figures fixes coverage. Annual reports are too long for an AI to read in one go, so reading the text meant cutting it short, and the answer was often in the part that was cut. With the tagged figures, both models almost stopped giving up. Claude improved by 48 percentage points (95% CI 38.2–57.8).

      The model still matters. With exactly the same evidence, GPT-5.6 Terra was wrong on 18.2% of the questions it answered and Claude on 5.1%.

      Asking the same question several times didn't catch the mistakes. The wrong answers were consistent, so repeating the question gave the same wrong number. That's why TagTrace checks answers against the filing instead.

      Limitations: a small set of filings, three repeats per consistency check, and results that may not carry over to every filing. This site uses a version of the method with structured answers and server-side checks, so it isn't exactly the benchmarked setup.
